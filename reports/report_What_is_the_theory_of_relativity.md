## Key Findings

### 1. What is special relativity?

**Confidence:** 🟢 High

Special relativity is a theory of spacetime structure proposed by Albert Einstein in 1905, applying to physical phenomena in the absence of gravity. It is based on two postulates: the laws of physics are the same in all inertial frames, and the speed of light in a vacuum is constant. This theory leads to effects such as time dilation, length contraction, and mass-energy equivalence (E=mc²), and it establishes the speed of light as the maximum possible speed for any object.

### 2. What is general relativity?

**Confidence:** 🟢 High

General relativity is a geometric theory of gravity developed by Albert Einstein, published in May 1916. It describes gravity as a curvature of spacetime caused by mass and energy, as expressed by the Einstein field equations. The theory generalizes special relativity and refines Newton's law of universal gravitation, providing the modern accepted description of gravitation for macroscopic objects.

### 3. What are the experimental confirmations of the theory of relativity?

**Confidence:** 🟢 High

Experimental confirmations of the theory of relativity include the motion of the perihelion of Mercury, which was an early success of general relativity. Additional confirmations come from time dilation effects observed in particle accelerators and GPS satellites, where relativistic corrections are necessary for accuracy. The Michelson–Morley experiment also provided foundational evidence for special relativity by failing to detect an ether wind. No well-controlled prospective experiment has disproven relativity.
