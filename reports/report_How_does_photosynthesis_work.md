## Key Findings

### 1. What are the light-dependent reactions of photosynthesis and how do they convert light energy into chemical energy?

**Confidence:** 🟢 High

The light-dependent reactions of photosynthesis convert light energy into chemical energy by using pigments like chlorophyll to absorb photons. This absorption excites electrons, which are then transferred through an electron transport chain, driving the synthesis of ATP and NADPH. These energy-carrier molecules store chemical energy for use in the light-independent reactions to produce glucose.

### 2. What is the Calvin cycle and how does it fix carbon dioxide into organic molecules?

**Confidence:** 🟢 High

The Calvin cycle, also known as the light-independent reactions, is a series of biochemical processes that convert inorganic carbon dioxide into organic molecules such as glucose. It occurs in the stroma of chloroplasts and uses ATP and NADPH produced by the light-dependent reactions to power the three main phases: carboxylation, reduction, and regeneration of ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate (RuBP).

### 3. How do chloroplasts and photosynthetic pigments (e.g., chlorophyll) capture light energy?

**Confidence:** 🟢 High

Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll and other photosynthetic pigments embedded in their thylakoid membranes. These pigments absorb photons from sunlight, exciting electrons to higher energy states. The excitation energy is then transferred to reaction centers, where it initiates photochemical reactions that split water molecules, release oxygen, and convert light energy into chemical energy. This energy is subsequently used in the Calvin cycle to fix carbon dioxide into organic molecules such as sugars.
